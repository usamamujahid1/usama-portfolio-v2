From Drawings to Decisions: My Journey in Construction Estimation

Introduction

I’m Usama Mujahid, a BS Civil Engineering student at COMSATS University Islamabad, Abbottabad Campus. Alongside my degree, I’m building a professional foundation in construction estimation, quantity takeoff, and digital construction workflows — the discipline of pricing a building before a single brick is laid.

Entering the World of Construction Estimation

Construction is not only about building structures; it is equally about planning, accuracy, and managing resources well. What drew me to estimation was a simple realization: every project decision — budgets, schedules, material orders — traces back to how accurately a set of drawings is converted into quantities and costs.

As a Construction Estimation Intern at BYH, I put that into practice daily. I analyze architectural and structural drawings and prepare quantity takeoffs for real construction works, including brickwork, tiling, concrete, and finishing items — then translate those quantities into cost estimates that clients and project teams can rely on.

Skills and Digital Tools

I work with a modern toolkit built for efficient, verifiable estimating:

  • PlanSwift for digital quantity takeoff
  • Microsoft Excel for cost calculations, rate analysis, and reusable estimation sheets
  • AutoCAD for reading and interpreting construction drawings
  • AI tools (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) for research, cross-checking assumptions, and documentation

My approach is to combine traditional civil engineering fundamentals with modern digital technologies — particularly BIM and artificial intelligence — so that every estimate is faster to produce and easier to defend.

Future Vision

Construction is moving toward data-driven decision making, and estimation sits at the center of that shift. My goal is to become a professional Construction Estimator and BIM Engineer — someone who connects 3D models directly to cost data and integrates technology into construction planning and cost management from day one.

I believe continuous learning, practical experience, and a willingness to adopt new tools early are what separate engineers who follow the industry from those who help move it forward.
